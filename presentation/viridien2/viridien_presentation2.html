Forward Problem: Wave Propagation

Predicting seismic wavefield \(\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{x}, t)\) given Earth parameters \(\mathbf{m}(\mathbf{x})\) and source \(\mathbf{s}(\mathbf{x}, t)\).
1. Wave Equation
\begin{equation} \frac{\partial^2 u(\mathbf{x},t)}{\partial t^2} = c^2 \nabla^2 u(\mathbf{x},t) + s(\mathbf{x},t) \end{equation}
2. Matrix Operator Form Virieux & Operto 2009, Eq. 1
\begin{equation} \mathbf{M}(\mathbf{x}) \frac{\partial^2 \mathbf{u}(\mathbf{x}, t)}{\partial t^2} = \mathbf{A}(\mathbf{x})\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{x}, t) + \mathbf{s}(\mathbf{x}, t) \end{equation}
Acoustic Case (Scalar Field):
\(\mathbf{M} = 1/c^2\) and \($\mathbf{A}$ = \nabla^2\)
Elastic Case (Vector Field) -> Navier-Cauchy Equation:
$\mathbf{M}$ is mass density, $\mathbf{A}$ is stress-divergence operator (Lamé parameters).

3. Fourier Transform (Inhomogeneous Helmholtz equation) Virieux & Operto 2009, Eq. 2
\begin{equation} \mathbf{B}(\mathbf{x},\omega)\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{x},\omega) = \mathbf{s}(\mathbf{x},\omega) \end{equation}
Impedance matrix: $\mathbf{B}(\mathbf{x},\omega) = -\omega^2 \mathbf{M}(\mathbf{x}) - \mathbf{A}(\mathbf{x}) \equiv \mathbf{B}(\mathbf{m})$, where $\mathbf{m}$ represents the model parameters.
4. Sampling Virieux & Operto 2009, Eq. 3
\begin{equation} \mathbf{d}_{\text{cal}} = \mathbf{P} \mathbf{u} = \mathbf{P} \mathbf{B}^{-1}\mathbf{s} \end{equation}
The projection (sampling) operator \(\mathbf{P}\) samples the wavefield \(\mathbf{u}\) at discrete receiver locations \(\mathbf{x}_r\).

Objective Function

$\mathbf{m}$ -> Forward modelling -> $\mathbf{d}_{\text{cal}}(\mathbf{m})$
The Least-Squares Norm (Misfit Function) Virieux & Operto 2009, Eq. 4
\begin{equation} C(\mathbf{m}) = \frac{1}{2} \| \mathbf{d}_{\text{obs}} - \mathbf{d}_{\text{cal}}(\mathbf{m}) \|^2 \end{equation}
Quantifying residual vector \(\Delta \mathbf{d} = \mathbf{d}_{\text{obs}} - \mathbf{d}_{\text{cal}}(\mathbf{m})\) between observed and calculated wavefields at receiver positions.
\begin{equation} C(\mathbf{m}) = \frac{1}{2} \mathbf{d}^\dagger \mathbf{d} \end{equation}
Frequency Domain Form, using conjugate transpose \(\dagger\)!
Next question: what direction we update $\mathbf{m}$ in model parameter space to minimize this residual?

Inverse Problem: Gradient via Sensitivity Matrix

Matrix Form of Misfit Gradient Virieux & Operto 2009, Eq. 9
\begin{equation} \frac{\partial C}{\partial \mathbf{m}} = -\Re \left\{ \mathbf{J}^\dagger \mathbf{d} \right\} \quad \text{where } \mathbf{J} = \frac{\partial \mathbf{d}_{\text{cal}}}{\partial \mathbf{m}} \end{equation}
The gradient \(\nabla_{\mathbf{m}} C\) is the product of the adjoint sensitivity matrix \(\mathbf{J}^\dagger\) and data residual vector \(\mathbf{d}\).
Step 1 — Differentiate via Chain Rule:
\(\frac{\partial C}{\partial \mathbf{m}} = \frac{1}{2} \left[ \left(\frac{\partial \mathbf{d}}{\partial \mathbf{m}}\right)^\dagger \mathbf{d} + \mathbf{d}^\dagger \left(\frac{\partial \mathbf{d}}{\partial \mathbf{m}}\right) \right]\)
Step 2 — Substitute Jacobian \(\frac{\partial \mathbf{d}}{\partial \mathbf{m}} = -\mathbf{J}\):
\(\frac{\partial C}{\partial \mathbf{m}} = -\frac{1}{2} \left[ \mathbf{J}^\dagger \mathbf{d} + (\mathbf{J}^\dagger \mathbf{d})^\dagger \right] \;=\; -\Re \left\{ \mathbf{J}^\dagger \mathbf{d} \right\}\)
The Computational Bottleneck
Perturbation: \(m_j \to m_j + \delta m_j\) → Wave propagates: \(\mathbf{B}(\mathbf{m}+\delta\mathbf{m})\mathbf{u} = \mathbf{s}\)
Forming the Jacobian \(\mathbf{J}\) explicitly requires \(N_m\) full wave simulations (one per grid cell, millions of runs!).
Solution: The Adjoint-State Method

The Adjoint State Method

Adjoint Wavefield \(\mathbf{r}_b\) & Gradient Formula Virieux & Operto 2009, Eq. 24
\begin{equation} \nabla_{m_l} C = \Re \left\{ \mathbf{u}^\dagger \left( \frac{\partial \mathbf{B}}{\partial m_l} \right)^\dagger \mathbf{r}_b \right\} \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad \mathbf{B}^\dagger \mathbf{r}_b = \mathbf{P}^\dagger \mathbf{d} \end{equation}
The Goal:
\(\frac{\partial C}{\partial m_l} = -\Re \left\{ \left( \frac{\partial \mathbf{d}_{\text{cal}}}{\partial m_l} \right)^\dagger \mathbf{d} \right\} = -\Re \left\{ \left( \mathbf{P} \frac{\partial \mathbf{u}}{\partial m_l} \right)^\dagger \mathbf{d} \right\} = -\Re \left\{ \left( \frac{\partial \mathbf{u}}{\partial m_l} \right)^\dagger \mathbf{P}^\dagger \mathbf{d} \right\}\)
Step 1 — Differentiate Forward Wave Equation \(\mathbf{B}\mathbf{u} = \mathbf{s}\):
\(\frac{\partial \mathbf{B}}{\partial m_l} \mathbf{u} + \mathbf{B} \frac{\partial \mathbf{u}}{\partial m_l} = 0 \implies \frac{\partial \mathbf{u}}{\partial m_l} = -\mathbf{B}^{-1} \left( \frac{\partial \mathbf{B}}{\partial m_l} \mathbf{u} \right)\)
Step 2 — Substitute & Define Adjoint Wavefield \(\mathbf{r}_b \equiv (\mathbf{B}^{-1})^\dagger \mathbf{P}^\dagger \mathbf{d}\):
\(\frac{\partial C}{\partial m_l} = -\Re \left\{ \left( -\mathbf{B}^{-1} \frac{\partial \mathbf{B}}{\partial m_l} \mathbf{u} \right)^\dagger \mathbf{P}^\dagger \mathbf{d} \right\} = -\Re \left\{ -\mathbf{u}^\dagger \left( \frac{\partial \mathbf{B}}{\partial m_l} \right)^\dagger \underbrace{(\mathbf{B}^{-1})^\dagger \mathbf{P}^\dagger \mathbf{d}}_{\mathbf{r}_b \implies \mathbf{B}^\dagger \mathbf{r}_b = \mathbf{P}^\dagger \mathbf{d}} \right\}\)
Step 3 — Zero-Lag Cross-Correlation Formula (2 Solves Total!):
\(\nabla_{m_l} C = \Re \left\{ \mathbf{u}^\dagger \left( \frac{\partial \mathbf{B}}{\partial m_l} \right)^\dagger \mathbf{r}_b \right\}\)
1. Forward Wavefield \(\mathbf{u}\)
Fired from source \(\mathbf{s} \rightarrow\) propagates forward in time into the Earth.
2. Adjoint Wavefield \(\mathbf{r}_b\)
Data errors \(\mathbf{d}\) at receivers \(\rightarrow\) back-propagated into the Earth as virtual sources!
3. Gradient \(\nabla C = \mathbf{u} \otimes \mathbf{r}_b\)
Zero-lag spatial cross-correlation of \(\mathbf{u}\) and \(\mathbf{r}_b\) gives sensitivity for ALL grid cells!

Parameter Update

Newton Optimization Step Virieux & Operto 2009, Eq. 7
\begin{equation} \Delta\mathbf{m} = -\left[ \frac{\partial^2 C(\mathbf{m})}{\partial\mathbf{m}^2} \right]^{-1} \frac{\partial C(\mathbf{m})}{\partial\mathbf{m}} \;=\; -\mathbf{H}^{-1} \nabla_{\mathbf{m}} C \end{equation}
The perturbation direction is opposite to the steepest ascent of the objective function (\(-\nabla C\)).

The Newton step scales \(\nabla C\) by the inverse Hessian \(\mathbf{H}^{-1}\) (curvature) to adjust step size in steep vs flat directions.
Step 1 — 2nd-Order Taylor Expansion of Misfit \(C(\mathbf{m} + \Delta\mathbf{m})\):
\(C(\mathbf{m} + \Delta\mathbf{m}) \approx C(\mathbf{m}) + \left[ \nabla_{\mathbf{m}} C(\mathbf{m}) \right]^\top \Delta\mathbf{m} + \frac{1}{2} \Delta\mathbf{m}^\top \mathbf{H} \, \Delta\mathbf{m}\)
Step 2 — Differentiate w.r.t. \(\Delta\mathbf{m}\) & Set to ZERO (Finding minimum):
\(\frac{\partial C(\mathbf{m} + \Delta\mathbf{m})}{\partial (\Delta\mathbf{m})} = \nabla_{\mathbf{m}} C(\mathbf{m}) + \mathbf{H} \, \Delta\mathbf{m} = 0\)
Step 3 — Solve for Parameter Update \(\Delta\mathbf{m}\) (Newton Step):
\(\mathbf{H} \, \Delta\mathbf{m} = -\nabla_{\mathbf{m}} C \implies \Delta\mathbf{m} = -\mathbf{H}^{-1} \nabla_{\mathbf{m}} C(\mathbf{m})\)

Parameter Update: Practical Methods

1. Gradient Descent
\(\Delta\mathbf{m} = -\alpha_k \mathbf{g}_k\)
Fast compute per step, but slow overall convergence.
Attenuation -> infinitesimal gradient -> illumination blur at depth.
2. Diagonal Hessian
\(\Delta\mathbf{m} = -\alpha_k \, \left[\text{diag}(\mathbf{H})\right]^{-1} \mathbf{g}_k\)
Equalizes source energy illumination at depth!
\(\mathbf{H}\) scales $\mathbf{g}$.
3. Gauss-Newton
\(\Delta\mathbf{m} = -\left[\mathbf{J}^\dagger \mathbf{J}\right]^{-1} \mathbf{g}_k\)
Deconvolves point-spread illumination blur; drops 2nd-order terms.
Iterative Conjugate Gradient! Heavy operation!
4. Quasi-Newton (L-BFGS)
\(\Delta\mathbf{m} \approx -\tilde{\mathbf{H}}^{-1}_{\text{LBFGS}} \, \mathbf{g}_k\)
Builds inverse Hessian approximation from past gradient history!
$\mathbf{g}_k \equiv \nabla_{\mathbf{m}_k} C$

Summary: The Complete FWI Mathematical Loop

1. Forward Modeling (Eq. 1–3): Solve \(\mathbf{B}(\mathbf{m})\mathbf{u} = \mathbf{s}\) to calculate wavefield \(\mathbf{u}\) and synthetic data \(\mathbf{d}_{\text{cal}} = \mathbf{P}\mathbf{u}\).
2. Data Misfit (Eq. 4): Compute data residual vector \(\mathbf{d} = \mathbf{d}_{\text{obs}} - \mathbf{d}_{\text{cal}}\) and misfit \(C(\mathbf{m}) = \frac{1}{2}\mathbf{d}^\dagger\mathbf{d}\).
3. Adjoint Wavefield (Eq. 24): Back-propagate residuals to solve adjoint wave equation \(\mathbf{B}^H \mathbf{r}_b = \mathbf{P}^\dagger \mathbf{d}\).
4. Update (Eq. 7, 9): Cross-correlate wavefields \(\nabla C = \Re\{\mathbf{u}^\dagger (\frac{\partial \mathbf{B}}{\partial \mathbf{m}})^\dagger \mathbf{r}_b\}\) and update \(\mathbf{m}^{(k+1)} = \mathbf{m}^{(k)} - \alpha \mathbf{H}^{-1} \nabla C\).
5. Iterate: Repeat steps 1–4 until misfit \(C(\mathbf{m}) < \epsilon\) converges to the optimal Earth model \(\hat{\mathbf{m}}\)!

Linearized Born Scattering & Jacobian Derivation

Step 1 — Wave Equation Perturbation:
Start with governing wave equation \(\mathbf{B}(\mathbf{m})\mathbf{u} = \mathbf{s}\) and receiver data equation \(\mathbf{d}(\mathbf{m}) = \mathbf{P}\mathbf{u}\).
Perturb model and wavefield: \(\mathbf{m} \rightarrow \mathbf{m}+\delta\mathbf{m}\), \(\mathbf{u} \rightarrow \mathbf{u}+\delta\mathbf{u}\):
\(\mathbf{B}(\mathbf{m}+\delta\mathbf{m})\,(\mathbf{u}+\delta\mathbf{u}) = \mathbf{s}\)
Step 2 — Scattered Wavefield Solution:
Keeping only first-order perturbation terms yields the scattered wave equation:
\(\mathbf{B}\,\delta\mathbf{u} = -\left( \frac{\partial\mathbf{B}}{\partial\mathbf{m}} \,\delta\mathbf{m} \right)\mathbf{u} \quad \implies \quad \delta\mathbf{u} = -\mathbf{B}^{-1} \left( \frac{\partial\mathbf{B}}{\partial\mathbf{m}} \,\delta\mathbf{m} \right)\mathbf{u}\)
Step 3 — Final Boxed Jacobian Identity:
Since data perturbation \(\delta\mathbf{d} = \mathbf{P}\delta\mathbf{u}\) and by definition \(\delta\mathbf{d} = \mathbf{J}\,\delta\mathbf{m}\), we obtain:
\(\mathbf{J}\,\delta\mathbf{m} = -\mathbf{P}\mathbf{B}^{-1} \left( \frac{\partial\mathbf{B}}{\partial\mathbf{m}} \,\delta\mathbf{m} \right)\mathbf{u}\)
Part 2 — Exact Hessian Matrix Derivation:
Gradient vector defined as:
\(\boxed{\mathbf{g} = \mathbf{J}^\dagger \left(\mathbf{d} - \mathbf{d}_{\text{obs}}\right)} \quad \text{with} \quad \mathbf{J} = \frac{\partial \mathbf{d}}{\partial \mathbf{m}}\)
Now differentiate once more with respect to \(\mathbf{m}\):
\(\mathbf{H} = \frac{\partial}{\partial \mathbf{m}} \left[ \mathbf{J}^\dagger \left(\mathbf{d} - \mathbf{d}_{\text{obs}}\right) \right]\)
Using the product rule:
\(\boxed{\mathbf{H} = \mathbf{J}^\dagger \mathbf{J} + \sum_i \left(d_i - d_{\text{obs},i}\right) \frac{\partial^2 d_i}{\partial \mathbf{m}^2}}\)
Gauss-Newton Approximation: Drops the 2nd-order term, leaving \(\mathbf{H}_{\text{GN}} = \mathbf{J}^\dagger \mathbf{J}\)!
